\section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Model and sampler}
The held-out comparisons use the \krea{} transformer~\citep{flux1kreadev2025}, the checkpoint loaded by the official \dype{} and \sega{} scripts, with the \fluxdev{} text encoders and autoencoder~\citep{flux2024} (\cref{app:impl}). A $4096^2$ image has $\N{c@ntgt}$ tokens, \N{c@tokratio} times the \N{c@ntrain} of a native $1024^2$ image ($s=\N{c@s}$ per side). Every held-out arm uses \N{c@steps} Euler flow-matching steps, distilled guidance 4.5, bfloat16, batch size one, the same per-prompt seeds and the capped flow shift $\mu=\N{c@muofficial}$ (\cref{sec:background}).

\paragraph{Prompts and the dev/held-out split}
Of the 195 evaluation captions of \afk{}~\citep{zhang2025diffusion4k}, rows 0--7 form a development set for variant and hyperparameter choices, and rows 8--194 the held-out set ($n=\N{ho@nprompts}$) for the main comparisons. We fixed this split after an eight-prompt pilot and before inspecting any held-out score. The decomposition hypothesis arose from the dev pilot and was tested only held out. From the second campaign on, a written gate decided which variants could leave the dev set: against \segaofficial{} on the eight dev prompts, a variant needed higher ImageReward and full-frame MUSIQ and a CLIP drop of at most 0.3, and at most two variants could advance per campaign. Dev numbers are selection evidence only (\cref{app:dev}). Seed~0 is used throughout; a seed-1 replication covers held-out rows 8--62 ($n=\N{so@n@tsb}$) for \segaofficial, \tempsb{} and \constancytm. This replication and the partial \constancy{} run use the first available rows of the fixed manifest, not random prompt subsamples.

\paragraph{Metrics}
\emph{Alignment and preference}: CLIP score~\citep{radford2021clip,hessel2021clipscore}, the scaled cosine $100\max(\cos,0)$ of ViT-L/14 embeddings, PickScore~\citep{kirstain2023pickapic} and ImageReward~\citep{xu2023imagereward}. These models resize their input to 224 pixels, so we read them as measures of layout and alignment (\cref{sec:pitfalls}). \emph{No-reference quality}: MUSIQ~\citep{ke2021musiq} and CLIP-IQA~\citep{wang2023clipiqa}, on the full $4096^2$ frame (``full-frame'') and averaged over four fixed $1024^2$ crops shared by all methods (``crop''). The pyiqa 0.1.16 MUSIQ pipeline combines 224- and 384-pixel views with every nonoverlapping $32^2$ native-resolution patch: 16,577 patches for a $4096^2$ frame, with no native-patch cap (\cref{app:impl}). Thus full-frame MUSIQ includes all original pixels but also differs from crop MUSIQ in spatial context and scale weighting. \emph{Spectral fidelity and repetition}: the root-mean-square and log-log slope errors of luminance power in 11 octave bands from $2^{-12}$ to $2^{-1}$ cycles per pixel, against the mean profile of the 195 \afk{} reference images, and a tiling index, the autocorrelation at a 1024-pixel lag minus that at 1536 pixels. FID and KID~\citep{heusel2017fid,binkowski2018kid} are too noisy at our sample sizes to rank methods (\cref{app:full}). No metric here measures 4K quality on its own, and we ran no human study.

\paragraph{Statistics}
Each comparison is a paired difference against \segaofficial{} over the same prompts and seed, with a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval over 2,000 prompt resamples. Better, worse and tie describe these nominal intervals: a tie means the interval covers zero, not equivalence. We also compute approximate two-sided centered-bootstrap $p$-values from the absolute null-centered mean differences and apply Holm correction separately across the paired comparisons in \cref{tab:main} and in \cref{tab:deltas_full}. Stars mark adjusted $p\le0.05$; intervals and colour remain nominal. The allocation's full-frame MUSIQ gain survives both families, whereas its CLIP-IQA gain and \segactm's small CLIP loss do not. These bootstrap tests provide approximate, rather than exact finite-sample, error control. The remaining descriptive tables retain nominal intervals.
