\paragraph{Native-resolution capability screen.}
We generated one $1024^2$ comparator for each of the eight existing development
captions, using the same model revisions, prompt-derived seed, 28-step sigma
schedule and guidance as the archived $4096^2$ SEGA images. At native resolution,
the deployed SEGA implementation has unit rotary gains and its base positional
mapping. All eight images, sidecars, saved initial transformer inputs and archived
4K references pass independent provenance checks. Different noise shapes are
uncoupled, and the deployed positional mapping and gains change with resolution.
These pairs therefore support an observational capability screen, not an isolated
causal effect of resolution.

Before seeing the native outputs, we fixed one primary caption predicate and
one secondary observation per caption, with two separately reported shop-text
checks. Two model reviewers, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, independently inspected
all 16 images in a randomized, condition-label-hidden presentation at a common
1024-pixel display size. Each saved its complete common-view record before
opening original-resolution images. Both then inspected five originals covering
every ambiguous or violated predicate and both text checks; clear observations
for the other eleven views were carried forward explicitly. Reviewer A also
inspected four recorded crops. This is model-based annotation, not a human
study, and visible detail can reveal resolution despite hidden labels.

No annotation disagreement was resolved or suppressed.

This screen provides no primary native-success/4K-failure example to motivate
the proposed semantic-loss intervention. We close that particular rationale
without a seed search or text-repair pivot. Eight selected development captions,
one draw per condition and two model readings cannot estimate a population
failure rate or exclude other resolution failures. No new method or quality
improvement is established, and the fresh confirmation set remains unused.
