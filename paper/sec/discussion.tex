\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What to report}
A method that changes the rotary base, the temperature and per-band gains at once should report a control that keeps its base and temperature and drops the rest; here that control leaves a gap of \hoa{tsb}{musiq} full-frame MUSIQ points and no detected reward-model gain. The allocation reduces excess fine-scale power, but the association with higher MUSIQ does not establish a perceptual mechanism. Claims that credit spectral or content adaptivity should be tested against this control with full-resolution metrics, because 224-pixel scorers cannot directly resolve the affected detail.

\paragraph{Small dev sets mislead}
Eight dev prompts were enough to reject clearly worse variants and too few for anything else. On the dev set \tempsb{} looked slightly better than \segaofficial{} on MUSIQ, yet held out it trails by \hoa{tsb}{musiq} points; the one variant that passed the second gate lost on PickScore and crop MUSIQ held out; and a rerun of \segaofficial{} on the same dev prompts moved MUSIQ by more than the margin of the one variant that passed the third gate (\cref{app:dev}). The held-out set has now been inspected and cannot serve as fresh confirmation for new variants.

\paragraph{Limitations}
We study one model family (\krea) at one square size, $4096^2$; other aspect ratios, sizes, backbones and video are untested. The development comparison in \cref{sec:checkpoint} changes both checkpoint and guidance. All metrics are automatic: the reward models see 224 pixels, MUSIQ and CLIP-IQA are learned proxies with their own biases, the spectral error uses a mean reference profile rather than a per-prompt target, and we ran no human study. MUSIQ includes the full native patch grid, but its full-frame and crop scores also differ in context and scale weighting. Most arms have one seed; the seed-1 replication covers two arms on the first \N{so@n@tsb} held-out prompts. The main paired tables include approximate Holm-adjusted tests, but intervals remain nominal and the descriptive tables have no multiplicity correction; \cref{tab:deltas_full} alone holds \N{ho@ncomp}. The original \constancy{} has only a partial held-out run. The paper-versus-code numbers come from an eight-prompt dev pilot and are descriptive, the mechanism probes use three dev prompts or fewer, and the CPU study of \sega{} uses another latent space. We port \sega{} and \dype{} from their released code with parity tests and ran the official \dype{} code only for \cref{sec:checkpoint}. Sampling cost (\cref{app:compute}) limited the number of arms, seeds and resolutions.
