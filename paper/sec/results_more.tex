\paragraph{The printed law is unbounded and scores worse}
Nothing bounds the allocation under the printed law (\cref{eq:sega,tab:papercode}). At $4096^2$ its physical bin map sends every pair with a wavelength above \N{c@tokside} tokens, \N{c@collapsedpairs} of the \N{c@pairsperaxis} per axis, to one frequency bin. The released law scales the spread by 0.15 and clamps the result at 1, so its logit gain stays between $\NmechSegaGainMin$ and $\NmechSegaGainMax$ on our held-out runs. In the eight-prompt dev pilot (descriptive only; \cref{app:pilot}), our implementation of the printed equations scores worse than the released code on every metric, with ImageReward \pim{segap}{ir} against \pim{sega}{ir} (\pid{segap}{ir} \N{pi@ci@segap@ir}). Frozen as a static tilt, the printed allocation spans gains of $\NmechSegaStaticGainMin$ to $\NmechSegaStaticGainMax$ and destroys images (CLIP \pim{segapst}{clip} against \pim{sega}{clip}), while the frozen release costs much less (CLIP \pim{segast}{clip}; \cref{fig:papercode}). Comparisons against \sega{} should therefore state which version they ran.

\subsection{Pitfalls in 4K evaluation}\label{sec:pitfalls}\label{sec:spectra}\label{sec:checkpoint}

\paragraph{224-pixel scorers miss the finest octaves}
CLIP, PickScore and ImageReward see a 224-pixel view with Nyquist frequency \N{nyq@cpp} cycles per original pixel ($2^{\N{nyq@log}}$). Bands \N{nyq@firstband} to 10 of our 11 octaves lie entirely above it, and the methods differ there. Band 8's log-power error against the references is \N{oct@tsb@b8} for \tempsb{} and \N{oct@sega@b8} for \segaofficial{} (\cref{fig:spectrum_main,tab:octave}), which may help explain reward-model ties next to a full-frame MUSIQ gap. Resizing can keep indirect effects, but these scorers cannot resolve original detail and do not suffice alone at 4K.

\paragraph{The lattice is a checkpoint effect}
Our first campaign used \fluxdev, on which \dype{} and \constancy{} produced a dense halftone lattice over the $4096^2$ frame (\cref{fig:checkpoint}), but that comparison also changed guidance. With the official \dype{} code at matched guidance, \fluxdev{} carries \N{st@k@ratio@g3p5}$\times$ \N{st@k@ci@g3p5} the \krea{} lattice power at guidance 3.5 and \N{st@k@ratio@g4p5}$\times$ \N{st@k@ci@g4p5} at 4.5 (8 dev prompts; \cref{tab:strengthen-ckpt}). Raising guidance scales the \fluxdev{} lattice by \N{st@k@two@gdev}$\times$ \N{st@k@twoci@gdev}, so guidance modulates the lattice, but the checkpoint gap remains at both values.
