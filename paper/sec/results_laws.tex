\subsection{Principled per-band laws do not beat \segaofficial}\label{sec:laws}

\paragraph{Outcome}
None of the four laws of \cref{sec:ours} improves on \segaofficial{} (\cref{tab:main}, bottom block). \constancytm{} ties it on ImageReward and PickScore and is worse on CLIP (\hod{ctm}{clip}), full-resolution MUSIQ (\hod{ctm}{musiq}), crop MUSIQ (\hod{ctm}{musiqc}), CLIP-IQA and the spectral error. It also loses to \tempsb, which shares its base and temperature and has no allocation: crop MUSIQ \hodci{ctmtsb}{musiqc}, full MUSIQ \hod{ctmtsb}{musiq}, with small gains in both spectral errors (\hod{ctmtsb}{spec} and \hod{ctmtsb}{slope}). \segactm{} at $\eta=1$, the only variant to pass the campaign-2 dev gate, ties \segaofficial{} held out on ImageReward (\hodci{sctm}{ir}) and full MUSIQ (\hodci{sctm}{musiq}) and loses on PickScore (\hodci{sctm}{pick}) and crop MUSIQ (\hodci{sctm}{musiqc}). \aliastemp{} and \aliastemps{} failed the gate on ImageReward and were never run held out (\cref{tab:dev}). A partial held-out run of the original \constancy{} is reported in \cref{app:partial}. Seed~1 repeats the \constancytm{} pattern (\cref{tab:seed1}). We find four reasons, each measured on the generated trajectories (\cref{fig:mechanism}).

\paragraph{The visibility gate opens too late}
\constancy's correction is exactly zero until a band with a wavelength of 16 to 64 tokens holds more than twice the noise power ($w>\tfrac12$). On the eight dev prompts of \cref{fig:mechanism}a the correction first becomes nonzero at $\sigma=\NmechConstDevOnsetSigmaHi$ and is nonzero on all of them only by $\sigma=\NmechConstDevOnsetSigmaLo$; the partial held-out run agrees (\cref{app:partial}). For most of sampling, including the steps that fix the layout, \constancy{} is the \ntk{} base with a log-$N$ temperature. A gate built on the exact noise floor is principled, but at $4096^2$ the bands it guards stay below that floor until late.

\paragraph{A native-resolution target trades sharpness for spectral match}
The time-matched target opens the gate early: on the held-out \constancytm{} runs, corrections exceed $\NmechMaterialThreshold$ in log gain after the first $\NmechTmMaterialStepMed$ steps (median), and $\NmechTmNegPct\%$ of all nonzero corrections are negative, lowering the mid pairs 6 to 8 ($\NmechTmCoarseFinal$ at the last step) and, late, the finest pairs 0 to 2 ($\NmechTmFineFinal$). Matching the native spectrum at each noise level thus damps the bands that carry 4K detail, and the held-out outcome follows: against \tempsb{} the spectral errors shrink slightly while full and crop MUSIQ fall. At this resolution, spectral agreement with native statistics and sharpness as MUSIQ measures it are different objectives.

\paragraph{\sega's allocation is mostly a fixed tilt}
Across the held-out prompts, the released logit gain stays between $\NmechSegaGainMin$ and $\NmechSegaGainMax$. A fixed tilt over pairs explains $\NmechSegaStaticPct\%$ of its variance and a tilt plus a per-step profile $\NmechSegaStaticTimePct\%$; the prompt-dependent remainder, $\NmechSegaContentPct\%$ overall and $\NmechSegaLateContentPct\%$ from step $\NmechLateFirstStep$ on, is an upper bound on content dependence because it also contains each prompt's noise draw. At the last step the finest pair has a logit gain of $\NmechSegaFinalFineGain$ and the $\NmechSegaFinalClampPairs$ slowest pairs per axis sit at the clamp of 1. A law that adapts more to content therefore has little to copy, and \segactm, which raises the prompt-dependent share to $\NmechCtmContentPct\%$, gains nothing held out. The CPU study of the printed equations in \cref{app:mech} finds the same structure on another latent space.

\paragraph{The actuator is weak and hard to predict}
Every law assumes that a band's gain raises its output power. A probe on three dev prompts that scales one octave's gain by $\NmechProbeFactor$ (\cref{fig:mechanism}b) finds a clear same-octave width-axis response for one prompt and a near-zero one for the other two, off-diagonal responses spread across octaves, and a weak height-axis response; on two dev prompts, a local linear model predicts half-step band-power changes little better than no change (\cref{app:mech}). These small diagnostics agree: gain is a blunt, prompt-dependent handle on band power, so a per-band law would need a measured response model of the network, not only a target for the image.
