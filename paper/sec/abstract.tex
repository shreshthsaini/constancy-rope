Diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel degrade when sampled at 4K. Their rotary position embeddings (RoPE) meet query-key offsets outside the training range, and each softmax spreads over sixteen times as many tokens. Training-free remedies either remap RoPE frequencies (position interpolation, NTK, YaRN, DyPE) or rescale attention logits, globally or, in the recent SEGA, per RoPE band from the spectrum of the noisy latent. We analyze SEGA's gain law and show that its per-band allocation is invariant to the slope of a power-law spectrum: it encodes the standardized shape of the band-energy profile, not how much detail is missing. We then derive a per-band attention gain from five findings of human spatial vision: Wiener-optimal coding under a known noise floor, contrast constancy, blur adaptation toward natural spectra, octave-band channels, and divisive normalization. The resulting method, Constancy RoPE, runs at every sampling step and on each spatial axis. It measures the half-octave power of the predicted clean latent, compares it with a profile recorded once from the model's own native-resolution samples, gates each band by its signal-to-noise ratio against the exactly known noise floor of the flow, and sets the logit gain of the RoPE dimensions in that band to a log-token-count temperature times a bounded correction. A spectrum that already matches the native profile receives the temperature alone.
