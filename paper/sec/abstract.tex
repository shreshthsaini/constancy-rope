\begin{abstract}
Training-free methods let diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel generate $4096\times4096$ images by editing the rotary position embedding: they change its frequency base, add a global attention temperature, and, in the recent \sega, scale each rotary band by a gain read from the latent spectrum. We ask which component produces the gain. On \krea{} at $4096^2$, with a dev/held-out split fixed in advance and paired bootstrap intervals over \N{ho@nprompts} held-out prompts, \sega's base and global temperature alone tie the released method on ImageReward, PickScore, CLIP score and crop MUSIQ and carry \shp{ir} \shci{ir} of its ImageReward gain over an NTK base; the spectral allocation adds \hoa{alloc}{musiq} full-resolution MUSIQ points. Four per-band laws derived from efficient coding and contrast constancy do not beat released \sega. We trace this to a visibility gate that opens too late, a native-resolution target that trades sharpness for spectral match, an allocation that is mostly a fixed tilt, and a gain actuator with a weak, prompt-dependent effect on band power. We also find that \fluxdev{} renders a halftone lattice at $4096^2$ under the official \dype{} code while \krea{} does not, that \sega's printed equations differ from its released code, and that 224-pixel reward models and the default flow-shift extrapolation silently distort 4K evaluations.
\end{abstract}
