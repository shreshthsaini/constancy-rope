\begin{abstract}
Training-free methods let a diffusion transformer trained near one megapixel generate $4096\times4096$ images by editing its rotary position embedding (RoPE): they raise the frequency base, add a global attention temperature and, in the recent \sega, rescale each rotary band by a gain read from the latent spectrum. We ask which edit produces the improvement. On \krea, with a dev/held-out split fixed in advance and paired bootstrap intervals over \N{ho@nprompts} held-out prompts, \sega's base and global temperature alone recover \shp{ir} \shci{ir} of its ImageReward gain over an NTK base and tie the released method on all three reward models and on crop MUSIQ; the per-band allocation adds only \hoa{alloc}{musiq} full-frame MUSIQ points. Four per-band laws that we derive from efficient coding and contrast constancy do no better: the two evaluated held out tie or lose to \sega{} on every reward and image-quality metric, and trajectory diagnostics show why. Results also depend on the checkpoint: \fluxdev{} breaks into a halftone lattice at $4096^2$ even under the official \dype{} code, while \krea{} stays clean. \sega's printed equations differ from its released code at every stage. Finally, 224-pixel reward models cannot see the top \N{nyq@nbands} octaves of a 4K image, and the default flow-shift extrapolation collapses the noise schedule.
\end{abstract}
