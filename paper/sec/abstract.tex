\begin{abstract}
Training-free edits of the rotary position embedding (RoPE) let diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel generate $4096\times4096$ images. Each new method changes several parts of it at once and credits the gain to the newest part. We introduce a controlled decomposition of \sega, a strong recent method with released code, into its frequency base, a global attention temperature and a per-band allocation. All arms share \krea, prompts, seeds and sampler. On \N{st@f@n} held-out prompts, a two-by-two factorial shows that the temperature carries \sega's whole ImageReward gain over an NTK base (\N{st@f@a@temp@ir} \N{st@f@ci@temp@ir}), while its base adds \N{st@f@d@base@ir} \N{st@f@ci@base@ir}. A preregistered test on \N{st@h@n@ir} prompts, \N{st@f@n} of them inspected earlier, finds base plus temperature equivalent to released \sega{} on two reward models. On the \N{st@e@n} fresh prompts alone, PickScore equivalence is not established, and the allocation adds \N{st@h@a@musiq} \N{st@h@ci@musiq} full-frame MUSIQ but no Holm-surviving reward-model gain. Four per-band laws from efficient coding and contrast constancy do not beat released \sega. New per-band methods should therefore be tested against a base-plus-temperature control. \end{abstract}
