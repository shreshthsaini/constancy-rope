\begin{abstract}
Training-free edits of the rotary position embedding (RoPE) let diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel generate $4096\times4096$ images. Each new method changes several parts of the embedding at once and credits its gain to the newest part, so it is unclear which edit helps. We decompose \sega, a strong recent method with released code, into its frequency base, a global attention temperature and a content-adaptive per-band allocation. All arms share \krea, prompts, seeds and sampler; a dev/held-out split was fixed in advance, and every comparison is paired over \N{ho@nprompts} held-out prompts with bootstrap intervals. \sega's base and global temperature alone recover all of its ImageReward gain over an NTK base (\shp{ir}, interval \shci{ir}) and tie the released method on three reward models and crop MUSIQ. A tie means the interval covers zero. The allocation adds \hoa{alloc}{musiq} full-frame MUSIQ points alongside reduced excess fine-scale power, without an established causal link. Four controllers derived from efficient coding and contrast constancy do not help: the two evaluated held out tie or lose to released \sega{} on every reward and image-quality metric. Code, per-image scores and table scripts are available at \codeurl.
\end{abstract}
