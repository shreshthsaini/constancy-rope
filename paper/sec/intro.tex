\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Diffusion transformers trained at about one megapixel are now asked for $4096\times4096$ images, which hold \N{c@tokratio} times more tokens than any training image. Training-free methods handle this by editing the rotary position embedding (RoPE)~\citep{su2024roformer} at inference, without new weights or extra sampling passes. They rescale its frequencies~\citep{chen2023pi,bloc97ntk,peng2024yarn}, schedule the rescaling with the noise level~\citep{issachar2025dype} or sharpen attention with a global logit temperature~\citep{jin2023training,nakanishi2025ssmax}. The most recent methods also scale each rotary band by a gain read from the spectrum of the current latent~\citep{sega2026}. Each method is compared as a package with the previous one, and the improvement is credited to its newest component. This paper asks which of these edits actually produces the gain, and whether a per-band law with a principled basis can do better.

Package comparisons cannot answer that question, because each method turns several of the knobs in \cref{tab:knobs} at once. \sega{} raises the frequency base, adds a global temperature and adds a content-adaptive per-band allocation in one step, and its paper credits the improvement to the allocation. A comparison against \dype{} or an NTK base cannot tell whether the allocation, the temperature or the base did the work. The same confound affects any new per-band law that is tested only against complete methods. Evaluation adds a second difficulty: the reward models used to rank these methods resize their input to 224 pixels, so they cannot resolve the finest octaves of a 4K image. A gain that lives only in those octaves can therefore be invisible to the metrics that decide which method wins.

We introduce a controlled decomposition of \sega~\citep{sega2026}, a strong recent method with released code, on the checkpoint its authors use (\krea). The decomposition control, \tempsb, keeps the released frequency base and applies \sega's reference logit gain $s^{0.16}$ to every query and key dimension, where $s=\N{c@s}$ is the scale per side. It drops only the per-band allocation, while an \ntk{} base with neither temperature nor allocation serves as the floor. All arms share prompts, seeds, schedule and sampler, and they differ only in the frequencies and gains of the attention logit. We fixed a dev/held-out split before any held-out score was seen, pair every comparison over \N{ho@nprompts} held-out prompts with bootstrap intervals, and replicate two arms at a second seed. We then ask whether per-band laws with a principled basis improve on \sega's heuristic. From efficient coding and contrast constancy we derive four controllers, select variants on the dev set under a written gate and evaluate the survivors held out. We do not propose a new state-of-the-art method.

Base and temperature carry most of the gain (\cref{fig:hero}): of \sega's gain over the \ntk{} base, they carry \shp{ir} of the ImageReward gain (interval \shci{ir}), \shp{pick} of PickScore and \shp{musiqc} of crop MUSIQ. \tempsb{} ties the released method on all three reward models, where a tie means that the paired interval covers zero. The allocation adds \hodci{alloc}{musiq} full-frame MUSIQ points alongside less excess power at 8 to 32 pixel wavelengths. This association does not establish a cause, and MUSIQ is a learned quality proxy rather than a sharpness measurement. The principled laws do not help: the two that reached the full held-out set tie or lose to \segaofficial{} on every reward and image-quality metric. The closer one, \segactm, loses \hoa{sctm}{pick} PickScore and \hoa{sctm}{musiqc} crop MUSIQ points, and small trajectory diagnostics suggest reasons without identifying causal mediators. We also find that \sega's printed equations differ from its released code at each of six compared stages, and that the printed law scores worse in a dev pilot. Finally, a lattice failure on \fluxdev{} appears in a development comparison that also changes guidance, so checkpoint comparisons need matched settings.

To summarize, we decompose a strong training-free 4K method under a fixed held-out protocol and test principled alternatives to its allocation. We highlight our main contributions below.
\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce a base-plus-temperature control and a paired held-out protocol that attribute the gains of training-free RoPE methods at 4K to individual edits.
\item We derive four per-band controllers from efficient coding and contrast constancy and find that the two evaluated held out tie or lose to released \sega{} on every reward and image-quality metric.
\item We show that \sega's base and global temperature recover \shp{ir} \shci{ir} of its ImageReward gain over an \ntk{} base, while its allocation adds \hoa{alloc}{musiq} full-frame MUSIQ points.
\end{itemize}
