# Supervisor review of the Constancy RoPE paper (2026-10-18)

Reviewed: `paper/dist/constancy_rope_paper.pdf` (22 pages, body 9 pages), source in `paper/sec/*.tex`,
tables from `paper/tables/make_tables.py`, figures in `paper/figs/`, against `paper/PLAN.md`, `HANDOVER.md`,
`.agent/notes/results.md`, `.agent/notes/codex_brief_5.md` and `.agent/notes/prereg_strengthen.md`.
Pages were rendered at 110 dpi and the eight figures at 200 dpi and inspected.

## 1. Verdict

**Borderline at NeurIPS/ICLR main track; weak accept at TMLR or a strong workshop.** With the running
strengthening campaign plus the two cheap experiments in Section 5 below, this becomes a weak accept at a
main track as an analysis paper.

Decisive reasons:

1. **The controlled decomposition is real, careful and useful.** Pre-split dev/held-out, paired bootstrap
   over 187 prompts, parity-tested ports, every number regenerated by a script and audited. The community
   needs the finding that released SEGA's spectral allocation buys about 2 MUSIQ points and nothing on
   reward models. The paper-versus-code and checkpoint findings are also things people will cite.
2. **The question in the title is only half answered.** "Base and temperature carry the gain" bundles two
   knobs. No held-out arm separates them: there is no (SEGA base, no temperature) arm and no (NTK base,
   SEGA temperature) arm. The only evidence that temperature dominates is the 41-prompt partial Constancy run
   on a different base with a different temperature and a late-active correction (Appendix D.1). A reviewer
   will say the headline is a two-factor result reported as one factor. This is a 2-arm, ~20 GPU-hour gap.
3. **Breadth of evidence is thin for a findings paper.** One model family, one checkpoint, one resolution,
   one seed for most arms, one prompt source, 60 unadjusted comparisons, no human study, and the main
   negative-result mechanisms rest on 2 to 3 dev prompts. The running campaign fixes seeds, fresh prompts
   and a second family; it does not fix point 2, the guidance confound in the checkpoint result, or the
   absence of any human judgement at 4K.

## 2. Claims audit

Supported as stated: finding (i) at the level of "base + temperature versus allocation"; the per-band laws
tying or losing (ii); the FA2 timing and GB200 speedup (v); the 224-pixel Nyquist arithmetic (v); the
seed-1 agreement count; every number I spot-checked against the tables.

Problems, each with a fix:

- **Overclaim, mechanism.** Abstract: "trajectory diagnostics show why". The diagnostics are 8 dev prompts
  (gate onset), 3 prompts (transfer probe), 2 prompts (local linear model), 1 prompt (cross-checkpoint
  probe, confounded by guidance and code revision). Fix: "suggest why" in the abstract and intro; move the
  one-prompt cross-checkpoint sentence (Section 5.3, second paragraph) to Appendix F entirely.
- **Misinterpretation, "sharpens".** Section 5.1 and 6 say the allocation "sharpens the full frame as
  MUSIQ sees it" and intro (i) calls it a "sharpness gain". Figure A.3b and Table B.4 say the opposite:
  SEGA base + temp. has MORE power than released SEGA at 8 to 32 px (+0.39 in band 7). The allocation
  removes excess fine-scale power; MUSIQ rewards the removal, which reads as less speckle, not more
  sharpness. Fix: replace "sharpens" with "suppresses excess fine-scale power that MUSIQ penalizes" and
  make the spectrum figure carry that sentence (promote Figure A.3b to the main text if space allows).
- **Unstated measurement.** "Full-frame MUSIQ" on a 4096^2 image: the protocol never says what the pyiqa
  MUSIQ actually consumes (multi-scale resize, native patches, patch cap). If it resizes, the full-frame
  versus crop distinction is partly an artefact of the implementation. Fix: one sentence in Section 4 and
  Appendix E stating the exact input pipeline and pyiqa version, and a note on whether the native-scale
  branch sees all 4096 px.
- **Confounded checkpoint claim (iii).** FLUX.1-dev ran at guidance 3.5 and Krea at 4.5; the official-code
  evidence is one prompt and one seed per checkpoint; the 8-prompt spectra come from the authors' own
  pipeline. Fix: run the official DyPE code on the 8 dev prompts for both checkpoints at both guidance
  values (32 images, about 2 GPU-hours) and state what the DyPE paper itself reports on FLUX.1-dev.
- **Multiplicity.** 60 intervals in Table B.3, 30 in Table 3, none adjusted; the limitation is stated but
  the headline verdict cells are not shown to survive adjustment. Fix: mark in Table 3 which verdicts
  survive Holm over the table (the allocation MUSIQ interval [+0.96, +3.25] will; the SEGA + TM CLIP
  interval [-0.20, -0.00] and the base + temp. CLIP-IQA interval [-0.018, -0.001] will not), and say so in
  one sentence.
- **Post-hoc hypothesis.** The decomposition control (temp_only) was added in campaign 1 after the dev set
  showed Constancy ≈ temp_only. The held-out split protects the test, but the paper should say the
  hypothesis itself was formed on dev data. Fix: one clause in Section 4 ("the decomposition hypothesis
  arose from the dev pilot and was tested only held out").
- **Underpowered pre-registration (E1).** H1 demands the IR CI inside ±0.05 with n = 150. The seed-0 CI at
  n = 187 is [-0.016, +0.070], width 0.086; at n = 150 expect width about 0.095, so H1 passes only if the
  fresh point estimate lands within about ±0.005 of zero. As registered, H1 is nearly certain to come back
  "unresolved" and the run then tells the reader nothing new. Fix (append as a dated amendment, before any
  fresh score is seen): either pool fresh150 with the held-out seed-0 prompts for the equivalence test
  (n = 337) or set the margin from a meaningful effect (the DyPE-minus-SEGA gap is 0.16 IR; ±0.08 is
  defensible). PickScore ±0.10 is fine.
- **"At every stage"** (Table 2, abstract). Six stages were compared, from "our reading of the paper".
  Fix: "at each of the six stages we compared", cite the exact commit hash and equation numbers in
  Appendix E, and record whether the SEGA authors were asked to confirm.
- **Flow-shift pitfall** is arithmetic with no image evidence. Fix: 8 images under the diffusers default
  schedule in Appendix A (about 0.5 GPU-hours) or soften to "we did not measure its effect on quality".
- **Selection of hero row 28** is justified in `f1_hero.py` ("chosen by subject before any output was
  viewed") but not in the caption. Fix: add the clause to the caption.
- **Partial Constancy inference** ("most of the base-and-temperature share is therefore temperature",
  D.1) rests on n = 41, non-random rows, a different base and temperature. Fix: label as suggestive and
  replace with the factorial arms (Section 5).
- **Abstract wording.** "recover 104% [97, 113]" reads as an error to a first reader. Fix: "recover all of
  its ImageReward gain (104% [97, 113])". "Tie" is used before it is defined; add "(interval covers zero)"
  at first use.
- **Seed-1 and partial rows are the first k rows, not random.** State this once in Section 4.

## 3. Figures and tables (art-director pass)

Overall: one Helvetica face, consistent method palette, hairline frames, CIs everywhere, captions that
lead with the finding. This is already above DyPE and UltraImage and level with the better NVIDIA report
figures. Two system-level issues: (1) the tables use blue = better and orange = worse while the figures use
blue = SEGA lineage and orange = Constancy-TM, so colour means two things in the same paper; (2) Table 3
and Figure 3 sit on one page and show the same 30 numbers twice.

- **Figure 1 (hero).** Claim is legible in 3 seconds from the panel titles, which is the point of a hero.
  Weaknesses: the crops in (a) show different content per arm (compositions differ), so the eye compares
  stained-glass windows rather than methods; panel (b) draws shares above 100% as bars that overshoot the
  SEGA line with a "dark segment" that is invisible for three of four rows; the crops are 0.8 in tall at
  print size. Highest-impact change: in (b) plot the two paired gains (base + temp., allocation) as stacked
  absolute bars with CIs per metric instead of a share, so 104% becomes a visible negative sliver, and in
  (a) choose a prompt where the SEGA and base + temp. crops contain the same object.
- **Figure 2 (method).** Handsome and in paper notation, but editorial labels ("big lever", "fine
  adjustment") state the result inside the method diagram; the right-hand logit curve overlays three
  lines that cannot be told apart at print size; panel (c) packs equations at 6.5 pt. Highest-impact
  change: drop the editorial labels, enlarge the logit-curve panel and simplify (c) to two labelled
  arrows into g_j with the equations left to the text.
- **Figure 3 (forest).** The best quantitative figure in the paper: direction shading, hollow seed-1
  markers, off-axis values printed. Weaknesses: the better-direction shading is nearly invisible, the
  legend is 6.5 pt grey, SEGA + TM has no seed-1 marker (say so), and it duplicates Table 3 above it.
  Highest-impact change: keep the forest in the main text, move Table 3 to the appendix, and use the
  recovered half page for the spectrum panel (A.3b) that explains the MUSIQ gap.
- **Figure A.1 (qualitative).** Well built (fixed rows, native crops, identical coordinates), but because
  compositions differ it reads as six different images per row; DyPE's crop A on row 8 is empty sky. A
  NeurIPS reviewer will expect a qualitative grid in the main body. Highest-impact change: cut to two rows
  with crop A chosen per row as the across-arm highest-detail tile where every arm has content, and move it
  to the main text at half height.
- **Figure 4 (checkpoint).** Clearest figure: the lattice is unmistakable and the whitened spectrum peak
  nails it. The "guidance 3.5 / 4.5" header exposes the confound. Highest-impact change: a matched-guidance
  row (see audit), then move the 8-prompt peak ratios from panel text into the caption.
- **Figure A.2 (paper vs code).** The released-SEGA heatmaps are blank on a ±1.5 shared scale, so the
  reader cannot see the tilt the text describes; the paper-equation panels saturate. Highest-impact change:
  add a line panel of mean log gain per pair for the four variants, keep one heatmap pair on a narrow scale.
- **Figure F.1 (mechanism).** (a) is clean; (b) is dense: dots for "same sign in 3 prompts" and boxed
  diagonal values compete, and n = 3 is hidden in the caption. Highest-impact change: show three small
  per-prompt width matrices instead of one mean, which also makes the "prompt-dependent" claim visible.
- **Figure A.3 (spectrum).** Good; the 8 to 32 px box is the single most important evidence in the paper
  for the MUSIQ story. Highest-impact change: draw the 224-view Nyquist as a labelled vertical line at
  2^-5.2 instead of a box, and promote panel (b) to the main text.
- **Tables.** Table 3 is exemplary (CIs below, triangles, groups). Table 1 is good; the Evaluated column
  should say n. Table D.1 is dense but legible. Table G.1 could drop the FA2 pilot rows to the text. Page 13
  is a nearly empty references page; acceptable for arXiv, fix with \raggedbottom or tighter refs for a
  camera-ready.

## 4. Writing

Abstract: dense, number-heavy, honest; first sentence is excellent. It ends on a list of pitfalls with no
takeaway. Add a closing sentence: "Claims for spectral or content-adaptive RoPE edits should be tested
against a base-plus-temperature control with full-resolution metrics." Intro: the five-item list is a
results section in disguise, which is fine for this venue; "Each method is compared as a package with the
previous one, and the improvement is credited to its newest component" is the best sentence in the paper
and should open the abstract's second sentence too. Structure and flow are good; Section 5.2 and the
Discussion are the honest core. Almost no filler; the recurring tic is triplets ("alignment, preference
and crop-level quality") and the word "therefore" after an interpretation. Nothing reads as machine
generated except the uniform density, which a human editor would vary. "Actuator" appears in the main
text without definition.

Worst five sentences and rewrites:

1. Abstract, 70 words: "On FLUX.1-Krea-dev, with a dev/held-out split fixed in advance and paired
   bootstrap intervals over 187 held-out prompts, SEGA's base and global temperature alone recover 104%
   [97, 113] of its ImageReward gain over an NTK base and tie the released method on all three reward
   models and on crop MUSIQ; the per-band allocation adds only 2.09 full-frame MUSIQ points."
   Rewrite: "On FLUX.1-Krea-dev, over 187 held-out prompts with paired bootstrap intervals, SEGA's base and
   global temperature alone recover all of its ImageReward gain over an NTK base (104% [97, 113]) and match
   the released method on every reward model. The per-band allocation adds 2.09 full-frame MUSIQ points and
   nothing else we can measure."
2. Section 3: "Relative to SEGA (released) it removes the allocation's shape, the small lift in mean gain
   that the clamp at 1 produces, and the difference on the index axis (gain 1 in the released code)."
   Rewrite: "It differs from SEGA (released) in three ways: no per-band shape, no lift in mean gain from the
   clamp at 1, and a gain of s^0.16 rather than 1 on the index axis."
3. Section 5.1: "SEGA's per-band allocation is therefore real but narrow: it sharpens the full frame as
   MUSIQ sees it and leaves alignment, preference and crop-level quality where the base and global
   temperature put them." Rewrite: "The allocation's effect is real but narrow. It removes excess power at
   8 to 32 pixel wavelengths (Figure A.3b), which full-frame MUSIQ rewards; reward models and crop metrics do
   not move."
4. Section 5.3: "Mechanism measurements need not transfer either: on one dev prompt, raising the gain of
   the fastest octave sharply lowers that band's power on FLUX.1-dev and slightly raises it on
   FLUX.1-Krea-dev, although guidance and code revision also differ between the two probes." Rewrite:
   delete from the main text; in Appendix F: "A single-prompt probe gave opposite signs on the two
   checkpoints, but guidance and code revision also differed, so we draw no conclusion."
5. Section 5.5: "Besides the flow shift of Section 2, two details changed what our numbers can show or
   what they cost." Rewrite: "Two further details limit what 4K numbers can show, and what they cost."

## 5. Missing experiments, ranked by impact per GPU-hour

1. **Factorial base × temperature, held out** (2 arms × 187: SEGA base with no temperature; NTK base with
   s^0.16; about 20 GPU-hours on GH200, 10 on GB200). Separates the two knobs the title promises to
   separate. Not in the current plan. Highest priority.
2. **Checkpoint claim at matched guidance** with the official DyPE code: 8 dev prompts × 2 checkpoints ×
   guidance {3.5, 4.5} (32 images, about 2 GPU-hours). Removes the one confound in finding (iii).
3. **Temperature sensitivity**: s^{0.08, 0.12, 0.20, 0.24} on the 55 seed-1 rows (about 12 GPU-hours).
   Shows whether SEGA's 0.16 is a tuned constant and how flat the optimum is, which is what makes
   "temperature is the lever" actionable.
4. **Fresh 150-prompt confirmation (E1, planned)**: good; fix the H1 margin first (audit above).
5. **Seeds 1 and 2 (E2, planned)**: good, but seeds for Constancy-TM and SEGA + TM (2 × 2 × 187 = 748
   images, about 40 GPU-hours) buy little; both are already negative with tight CIs. Spend that budget on
   items 1 and 3.
6. **Qwen-Image (E3, planned)**: right idea; keep it to three arms and add the base-only arm so the
   decomposition is replicated, not just the tie. Accept the "exploratory" label in the prereg.
7. **Small human study**: 2AFC on 50 held-out prompts, native 1024 crops at identical coordinates, SEGA vs
   base + temp. and SEGA vs NTK; zero GPU-hours, perhaps 6 person-hours. Top-venue reviewers will ask for
   any human signal at 4K, since the paper itself argues the automatic metrics are blind there.
8. **Diffusers-default flow shift**: 8 images (0.5 GPU-hours) to turn an arithmetic pitfall into a shown one.
9. **MUSIQ input audit**: zero GPU-hours; one paragraph.
10. **One other resolution** (2048^2 or 4096×2048, SEGA vs base + temp., 55 prompts, about 6 GPU-hours):
    tests whether the decomposition holds as s changes.

Does the running plan address the main weaknesses? Partly. E1 and E2 answer "one seed, one prompt set",
E3 answers "one family". None answers "base versus temperature", the guidance confound, or human
judgement. The prereg as written will most likely return H1 "unresolved" because the IR margin is tighter
than the attainable CI. Changes I would make to the brief: amend H1 before any fresh score exists; drop
seeds 1 to 2 for Constancy-TM and SEGA + TM; add the two factorial arms and the matched-guidance checkpoint
run to the 80_ queue ahead of E3; require the base-only arm on Qwen.
